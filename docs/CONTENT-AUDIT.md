# Content audit

Prepared 1 October 2026 from the files supplied in this workspace. This is an editorial audit, not a public biography. It separates evidence from proposals and deliberately excludes sensitive personal data.

## Direction supported by the material

The prepared material is much stronger as a **ministry journey and supporter-update site** than as a conventional career portfolio. Lina's recurring themes are faith becoming personal, service through relationships, learning across cultures, listening without pressure, and discerning the next step. Her work and education add texture and credibility, but should support this story instead of leading it.

Suggested public identity:

> **linway** — Lina's notes on faith, service, and learning along the way.

The tone in the source is earnest, calm, relational, and direct. It becomes less natural when it sounds like fundraising copy or a programme brochure. Use first person, short paragraphs, concrete experiences, and restrained theological language. Keep “I am learning” more often than “I have mastered.”

## Claims that need confirmation

The supplied website draft says, “I am spending the year in London” and describes Free English as an active placement. The dated programme material supports acceptance and planned dates, but there is no independent confirmation of arrival, participation, or assignment. Until Lina confirms, use “I was accepted for the 2026–27 programme” and “I hope to focus on cross-cultural ministry through Free English,” or omit the current chapter from the public preview.

The education material says she was approaching the end of university studies. It does not establish completion or a degree award. The experience page should say “Business and Management studies, Transport and Telecommunication Institute, Riga” without a graduation date until confirmed.

The draft describes the year as “self-funded.” The application describes savings and potential support, while the programme agreement also mentions a financial contribution from the church and eligible expenses. Use a neutral phrase such as “I am raising support for the practical costs of the year” only after Lina confirms the current arrangement. Do not publish a total, progress meter, or “fully self-funded” claim without evidence.

The original draft named Stewardship without a URL. On 1 October 2026, the site owner supplied `https://www.stewardship.org.uk/partners/20645926` as Lina's donation destination; the live page metadata names Lina Makarenko. It is suitable as a direct link in the private preview. Do not infer a fundraising target, fees, beneficiary wording, tax treatment, or current financial need from the link. Review those details before public deployment.

The draft asks visitors to leave an email address, but no mailing provider, consent text, retention policy, unsubscribe process, or list owner is supplied. For v1, link to a confirmed channel or show an honest “updates will appear here” message. Do not render a nonworking subscription form.

## Privacy exclusions

Never publish or pass into page data:

- home addresses, private phone numbers, birth date, signatures, passport/visa/sponsorship identifiers, or application reference data;
- private email addresses or contact details for referees, employers, pastors, staff, family, or third parties;
- medical or disability answers;
- immigration status inferred from sponsorship paperwork;
- financial commitments, savings, or private support arrangements;
- complete application, agreement, sponsorship, or prayer-letter originals;
- stories about identifiable young people, clients, colleagues, or ministry participants without explicit consent.

The two-page prayer/support letter could eventually be rewritten and exported as a separate sanitized update, but the supplied PDF must not become a website download. Review document metadata as well as visible text before publishing any derivative PDF.
